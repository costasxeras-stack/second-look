Product teardown · Allegro · car parts search

The query said "Golf 7". The results did not hear it.

I searched Allegro the way a driver does: "klocki hamulcowe golf 7", brake pads for a Volkswagen Golf Mk7. I logged eight queries on 21 November 2026 and counted what came back. Search finds the right category and handles typos well. It does not read the car from the query, so the shopper has to fill in the vehicle filter or check every listing by hand. This is the teardown, and a redesign.

Test log: eight queries

    What I found

    Confirmed in the test logThe car in the query barely changes the results. "golf 9", a generation number not on sale at the time of the test, returned 11,517 offers with no warning, more than "golf 7" (10,098). Only 4 of the 60 results on page 1 for "golf 7" name the Golf 7 in the title. The vehicle filter above the results stays empty.
    Confirmed in the test logPromoted listings titled for other cars sit near the top. A promoted front and rear pad set whose title names Golf 5 and 6 and Passat, not Golf 7, appeared at position 4 for "golf 7", at positions 1 and 2 with a typo or "golf 9", and in the top 4 for the bare query.
    Confirmed in the test logWords for position work, the car model does not. Adding "przód" (front) moved page 1 from 28 front and 15 rear titles to 37 front and 1 rear.
    Confirmed in the test logPart number search finds the part, then pads the list. "0986494660" puts the exact Bosch part first, then shows 42 other part numbers on page 1 and a count of 9,419 offers.
    Confirmed in the test logEnglish queries are much less precise. "brake pads golf 7" returned 56,862 offers, 5.6 times the Polish query, with brake discs and kits among the promoted results.
    Hypothesis, not testedThe other part numbers may be deliberate equivalents. If Allegro maps the part to compatible parts, the long list is a feature. The page does not say so, which is the problem either way.
    Hypothesis, not testedShoppers who skip the filter return more parts. Wrong-fit purchases are a known cost in car parts e-commerce. I cannot see Allegro's return data, so the size is [unknown].

    What changes

    1. Parse the vehicle from the query (brand, model, generation in numbers or Roman numerals) and pre-fill the filter as a chip the shopper can remove. Ask only for what the query cannot tell, usually the engine.
    2. Label fit on every card: fits, does not fit, or check engine. Even before the engine is known, generation alone rules out many parts.
    3. Hold promoted listings to the same fitment rule. An ad for a Golf 5 part should not outrank a Golf 7 part for a Golf 7 query.
    4. Say when a model is not recognised. "We do not know a Golf 9. Did you mean Golf 8?" is better than 11,517 silent results.
    5. Split exact part number matches from equivalents with a heading, so 9,419 offers become "1 exact part, [n] equivalents".

    I would test vehicle parsing and fit labels first, as an A/B test on car parts queries that name a model. The fitment rule for ads is a business decision and needs its own test.

    Method and limits

    • Eight queries, run once each on 21 November 2026 on allegro.pl in desktop Chrome, signed in, with the interface set to English. Offer titles appear machine-translated. Results can differ by account, location, time and device.
    • Counts come from the page: the offer count in the header and the titles of the first page of results. A title that does not name the Golf 7 may still fit it. That is the point: the shopper cannot tell from the results page.
    • I did not fill the vehicle filter, buy anything or contact sellers. I have no access to Allegro data. Allegro is not involved in this teardown.
    • Why this matters to me: I work on search for an automotive parts catalogue, where fitment is the hardest part of relevance.
